🖼️ The Outreach Graphic

One image to attach to your DMs and emails. The job of this graphic isn't to sell — it's to make the message feel personal, warm, and considered. Like a little invitation, not a flyer.

DM Attachment — A Cozy Invitation

Concept: "Here's a tiny piece of our world — come make one with us"

The feeling we want them to have in 3 seconds

"Oh, this is sweet. This studio feels like a real place run by real people. I want to be there."

Size

  • Square, 1080 × 1080 px — fits perfectly in Instagram DMs and email

The hero shot (most important part)

  • A hand cradling one tiny finished piece — a mini matcha bowl, a mug, or a ceramic earring
  • Shoot from slightly above, soft natural light from a window (golden hour is gorgeous)
  • Keep the background simple and out-of-focus — a soft wooden table, a linen cloth, or a clean studio surface works
  • The hand should feel relaxed and warm — fingertips lightly holding, not gripping. Photograph your actual hand or a friend's, not a stock image
  • This shot does the heavy lifting: shows scale, signals "handmade," and feels intimate. Use your existing brand palette here

Layout (top to bottom)

  1. Top 60%: The hero shot (hand + tiny piece). Lots of breathing room.
  2. Middle: The headline — short, warm, hand-written feel
  3. Below headline: 1–2 lines of friendly body copy
  4. Bottom: The deadline, sized small but clear
  5. Bottom corner: "@minipotterystudio" in a subtle, small text — so they remember who it's from

Type & feel

  • Use a soft serif (think Cooper, DM Serif Display) OR a rounded sans (think Quicksand, Nunito) — anything that feels gentle, never sharp or corporate
  • Generous spacing — let the design breathe. Cozy ≠ crowded.
  • One tiny accent — a small heart, a sparkle, or a hand-drawn doodle — adds warmth without clutter. Optional.
  • Use your existing brand palette (no need to overthink — soft and warm wins)

Text on the graphic

a tiny invitation 🤎 We'd love to host you at the studio for a free class. In return — help us capture a few moments we'll share on our gram. ──────── Tell us you're in by June 20 @minipotterystudio

What to leave OUT

  • No "FREE CLASS" in all caps — too transactional
  • No long bulleted lists on the image — that's what the DM message is for
  • No big "APPLY NOW" button — the form link goes in the DM text, not the image
  • No multiple pottery pieces in a grid — one piece, one hand, one moment
Why this approach: Nano creators get plenty of "free product" pitches. Most of them are slick and templated. A graphic that feels like a personal invitation — quiet, warm, hand-made — is what makes you stand out. The visual signals what the experience will feel like.

⚠️ Influencer Collab Rules & Red Flags

Things to know before you confirm a creator — protects you legally and helps avoid wasted classes.

1. Get content + likeness rights in writing 📝 (most important)

Since the creator is making content for YOU to post, you need explicit written permission covering:

  • Content license — right to use the Reel + photos on @minipotterystudio's Instagram, TikTok, website, and email marketing
  • Likeness release — right to show their face/hands/voice in the content (if they choose to appear)
  • Commercial use — explicit OK to use the content in ads or paid promotion (even if you don't plan to right now)
  • Term — how long you can use it. Best practice: in perpetuity, with credit
  • Edits — your right to crop, recut, colour-correct, or use in collages
What you should do: The confirmation email above includes these as a reply-YES agreement. Save their "yes" reply. For higher-stakes creators (or if you ever pay them in cash), use a 1-page content license document instead of email.

2. Disclosure — your responsibility, not theirs 🇨🇦

Since YOU'RE posting the content (not the creator), you're the one disclosing the partnership in your captions.

What works in your @minipotterystudio captions:

  • "📸 / 🎥 by @[creator]" — clear, friendly creator credit
  • "In collaboration with @[creator]" — for fancier framing
  • Tag the creator in the post and Story re-shares

Strictly speaking, when you're posting on your own brand account about your own brand, you don't need #Ad — you're not endorsing an external brand. But you DO need to clearly credit the creator. That's both ethical and helps build their portfolio.

3. Vet the creator before confirming 🔍

Green flags (good signs):

  • Comments from real people with full conversations (not just "🔥🔥" or "Love this!")
  • Engagement rate: 3–5% for nano creators (likes ÷ followers × 100). E.g. 5K followers + 150–250 likes per post = healthy
  • Their audience is Toronto-based — check Stories for local content/locations
  • Their aesthetic matches yours (cozy, handmade, lifestyle)

🚩 Red flags (skip them):

  • Low engagement (under 1%) — usually means fake/bought followers
  • Big follower count with tiny like counts — same red flag
  • Sudden follower spike in a short period — bought followers
  • Comments are mostly emojis or generic ("Nice post!") from accounts that all look the same
  • Recently posted a paid partnership with a direct competitor (Clay With Me, Pottery Dream, etc.)
  • Asks for cash on top of the free class — that's no longer "free class in exchange for content"
  • Wants to see "approval" from you on the content before posting — okay to negotiate, but be aware it adds delay

4. Set clear deliverable expectations 📋

In your confirmation email, be explicit about:

  • Exact format (1 Reel + 3 photos)
  • Minimum length (Reel: 60+ seconds)
  • Delivery method (Google Drive folder link from you)
  • Deadline (within 2 weeks of class)
  • File quality (originals, not screen-recorded)
  • Whether they want to appear in the content (face / hands-only / behind-camera)

5. Add a no-show / cancellation policy 🗓️

Each class costs you time + materials. Protect against no-shows:

  • Require 48+ hours notice for cancellation
  • 2 strikes (no-show or late cancel) and they forfeit future opportunities with you
  • Send a friendly reminder 24 hours before class

6. Track results 📊

Hard to know what's working unless you measure. Easy ways:

  • Give each creator a unique discount code (e.g. ERIKA15) so you see who drove bookings
  • Use a unique trackable link (bit.ly or Linktree analytics)
  • At minimum, check: views on Reel + follower growth on @minipotterystudio in the week after they post

7. Tax note for the creator 💰

In Canada, free goods/services received in exchange for promotion may be taxable income for the creator. For a $79–$200 class it's small, but it's their responsibility to track — not yours. You don't need to issue them anything (no T4A required at this value), but it's polite to mention it once.
